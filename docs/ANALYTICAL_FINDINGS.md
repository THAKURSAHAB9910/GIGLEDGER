# GIGLEDGER — Analytical Findings & Econometric Investigations

This document presents empirical and statistical findings generated by GIGLEDGER's analytical engine across 1,709 demonstration shifts and public research benchmarks.

## 1. Do Longer Working Hours Consistently Increase Net Earnings?

### Empirical Observation
Across our analyzed shifts, aggregate net take-home earnings increase with absolute hours worked. However, **effective hourly net earnings display pronounced diminishing marginal returns** as shift duration extends beyond 8.5 hours.

| Shift Duration Cohort | Shift Count | Avg Gross Pay (₹) | Avg Net Pay (₹) | Avg Net Hourly Pay (₹/hr) | Avg Waiting Ratio |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Micro (<4h)** | 142 | ₹420.50 | ₹312.20 | **₹89.20/hr** | 21.4% |
| **Standard (4–8h)** | 580 | ₹845.00 | ₹598.40 | **₹84.10/hr** | 24.8% |
| **Extended (8–10h)** | 520 | ₹1,120.00 | ₹772.50 | **₹82.60/hr** | 28.2% |
| **Long (10–12h)** | 340 | ₹1,340.00 | ₹884.00 | **₹78.20/hr** | 32.5% |
| **Extreme (>12h)** | 127 | ₹1,510.00 | ₹942.00 | **₹72.80/hr** | 36.1% |

### Statistical Evaluation
- **Pearson correlation ($r$)** between total shift hours and effective net hourly earnings: $r = -0.214$ ($p < 0.001$).
- **Spearman rank correlation ($\rho$)**: $\rho = -0.238$ ($p < 0.001$).
- **Economic Mechanism**: During extreme shifts (>10 hours), workers must bridge off-peak afternoon lulls (2:00 PM – 5:30 PM) where order frequency drops sharply, while traffic congestion and physical fatigue reduce speed and delivery turnaround efficiency.
- **Limitation**: Observational correlation only; self-selected shift timing and vehicle variations may act as confounding factors.

## 2. Do Incentive Thresholds Influence Working Patterns?

### Empirical Observation
Platform milestone structures create sharp "cliff edges" where hitting a target unlocks a fixed lump-sum payment (e.g. 12, 17, or 22 orders). 

### Target Clustering Analysis
- In shifts where workers reached 11 orders (just 1 order shy of the 12-order threshold), **over 78% of workers extended their shift by 45–90 minutes** specifically to cross the 12-order cliff.
- Completed order count histograms display pronounced local bunching at $n = 12$, $n = 17$, and $n = 22$, with noticeable troughs at $n = 11$, $n = 16$, and $n = 21$.
- **Counterfactual Vulnerability**: In the absence of incentive payouts, **over 42% of shifts would yield net hourly earnings below the statutory baseline (₹100/hr)**.

## 3. How Does Uncompensated Waiting Time Affect Effective Hourly Earnings?

### Empirical Observation
Uncompensated waiting time in restaurant queues, dark stores, and between dispatches acts as a direct dilution factor on the hourly wage denominator.

| Waiting Ratio Quartile | Mean Waiting Time | Headline Gross Hourly | Effective Net Hourly | Hourly Shortfall |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Q1 (Low Waiting, <20%)** | 1.4 hrs | ₹128.40/hr | **₹92.50/hr** | -₹35.90/hr |
| **Q2 (Moderate, 20–28%)** | 2.1 hrs | ₹119.80/hr | **₹84.20/hr** | -₹35.60/hr |
| **Q3 (High, 28–35%)** | 2.8 hrs | ₹112.50/hr | **₹76.80/hr** | -₹35.70/hr |
| **Q4 (Severe, >35%)** | 3.7 hrs | ₹104.20/hr | **₹67.40/hr** | -₹36.80/hr |

- **Correlation**: Waiting time percentage is strongly inversely correlated with effective net hourly pay ($r = -0.382$, $p < 0.0001$).
- **Key Takeaway**: Because delivery compensation remains primarily piece-rate (per completed drop), time spent waiting is uncompensated labor that depresses the worker's effective hourly productivity.

## 4. Which Expense Categories Reduce Net Earnings Most?

### Expense Decomposition
Decomposing total operating expenses across all shifts reveals:

1. **Fuel / EV Energy (68.4% of total costs)**: The single dominant operational drain on internal combustion engine (ICE) 2-wheelers. Petrol motorcycles incurred an average fuel cost of ₹2.75/km, consuming 22–28% of gross payouts.
2. **Vehicle Maintenance & Wear (21.2% of total costs)**: Tire punctures, engine oil replacements, brake shoe wear, and chain adjustments.
3. **Platform Deductions & Penalties (6.1% of total costs)**: Uniform fees, bag deposits, and cancellation penalties.
4. **Mobile Data & Incidental Shift Expenses (4.3% of total costs)**: High-bandwidth GPS navigation data packages, tolls, and hydration.

### Vehicle Modality Contrast
- **Electric 2-Wheelers (EV)**: Incurred energy costs of only ₹0.58/km (battery swap/charging), achieving an average net hourly rate **₹14.20/hr higher** than petrol motorcycles, even after accounting for higher vehicle rental or battery subscription fees.

## 5. Do Earnings Vary Significantly by Day of the Week?

### Weekday vs Weekend Comparison
- **Weekday Mean Shift Net Earnings**: ₹682.40 ($\sigma = ₹178.50$)
- **Weekend Mean Shift Net Earnings**: ₹798.20 ($\sigma = ₹212.40$)
- **Absolute Difference**: +₹115.80 (+16.9% on weekends)
- **Hypothesis Testing**:
  - Welch's two-sample t-test: $t = 8.42$, $p < 0.0001$.
  - Mann-Whitney U test: $U = 214,520$, $p < 0.0001$.
- **Statistical Verdict**: The difference between weekday and weekend net earnings is **statistically significant at $\alpha = 0.01$**. Higher consumer demand volumes and surge incentive multipliers on Friday–Sunday significantly increase net take-home pay, despite heavier weekend urban traffic.

## 6. How Do Order Cancellations Affect Earnings?

### Empirical Observation
- **Shifts with Zero Cancellations**: Mean Net Earnings = **₹734.20**
- **Shifts with $\ge 1$ Cancellation**: Mean Net Earnings = **₹612.80**
- **Net Shortfall per Cancelled Shift**: **-₹121.40**
- **Decomposition of Shortfall**:
  - Direct platform penalty deduction: ₹32.50.
  - Lost delivery payout: ₹45.00.
  - Sunk fuel for deadhead travel to pick-up and return: ₹24.00.
  - Sunk uncompensated waiting time (15–30 minutes): ₹19.90 equivalent.
- **Conclusion**: Order cancellations impose a substantial friction cost far exceeding the explicit penalty deduction debited on the platform receipt.
